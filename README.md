This repository contains the code used in my master's thesis in Theoretical and Applied Linguistics (*The inflection-derivation divide from a distributional semantics perspective: A case study in Polish aspectual morphology*).



**Abstract**: This thesis investigates the boundary between inflection and derivation through the lens of distributional semantics. In the first study, we examine Polish and Spanish morphology, comparing inflectional and derivational operations. Results show a distinction between the two.
However, when separating derivation in category-changing and category-preserving, there is no clear difference between inflection and category-preserving derivation, suggesting no binary distinction. The second study focuses specifically on aspectual morphology in Polish, in
order to further compare inflection and category-preserving derivation. We analyze so-called empty prefixes, prefixes that only change the aspect of the verb and do not alter the meaning of it. These prefixes are controversial in that they are considered inflectional by some and deriva-
tional by others. We examine them alongside aspectual suffixes, and derivational prefixes. Results reveal that they pattern closely with aspectual suffixes in terms of semantic similarity, while all derivational prefixes remain distinct.
